How to choose a Dell PowerEdge server

Four key decisions to get the right configuration for your workload.

1U or 2U?

1U servers (, , ) pack more compute per rack unit.

2U servers (, ) fit more drives, more PCIe slots and full-size GPUs, and are easier to cool.

1U — higher densityMore compute per rack unit
2U — more expansionMore drives, PCIe slots and GPU support

How much storage and which drives?

Start by counting the drive bays. Common layouts are 8 SFF, 16 SFF, 24 SFF or 12 LFF. Then choose the drive type.

SFF bays suit SSD and NVMe, where speed matters most.

LFF bays give the cheapest bulk capacity on large hard drives.

Common drive bay layouts
NVMeThe pick for databases, virtualization and anything latency-sensitive
SASFits most workloads
SATAKeeps the cost down
“xd” modelsStorage-optimized: they hold more drives in the same 2U chassis.

Intel or AMD?

AMD EPYC servers (, , ) offer high core counts and NVMe bandwidth, which suits virtualization.

Intel Xeon servers (, , , ) fit workloads tuned for Intel and per-core licensing.

Do you need GPUs?

In the 14G generation the is the usual GPU-capable choice. The 1U is not the right platform for full-size GPUs.

Use cases

The right PowerEdge model depends on the workload. Here is where each platform fits best.

Virtualization (VMware, Proxmox, Hyper-V)

Dual-socket servers with plenty of RAM and fast storage consolidate many virtual machines onto fewer hosts. Popular choices: for density, for more drive bays and expansion, and or for newer platforms.

Database servers

Databases need fast storage and memory bandwidth. NVMe chassis such as the R640 with 10SFF NVMe, the with up to 24 NVMe drives, or the (AMD EPYC) keep latency low under heavy load.

File, backup and archive storage

For capacity rather than speed, choose large-form-factor chassis: the R740xd with 12LFF, the R740 with 8LFF, or the R640 with 4LFF for smaller archives.

AI and GPU workloads

GPU-capable platforms for AI/ML inference, rendering and compute, including the PowerEdge R750xa and R7525.

VDI and remote desktop (RDP)

High core counts and large memory support many concurrent user sessions. The R640, R660 and R7525 are common choices.

Video surveillance (CCTV) storage

Continuous recording needs a lot of disk capacity. Chassis such as the R740xd 12LFF and R740 8LFF are built for it.

Small business and office servers

A compact, reliable server for file sharing, line-of-business applications and small virtualization hosts. The R640 and R650 with 4LFF, or the R660 with 8 or 10 SFF, fit most offices.

Which Dell PowerEdge generation should I buy?

For most production workloads, 14G (R640, R740) offers the best price and performance. Choose 13G for tight budgets and non-critical use, 15G for PCIe 4.0 and faster memory, 16G for DDR5 and PCIe 5.0, and 17G (R670, R770) when you want the newest Intel Xeon 6 platform.

What is the difference between Dell R640 and R740?

Both are 14th Gen with dual Xeon Scalable CPUs. The R640 is 1U and optimized for density. The R740 is 2U, with more drive bays, more PCIe slots and GPU support.
